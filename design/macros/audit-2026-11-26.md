# Audit of `rust/dialog-notation`, 2026-11-26

**Of:** `feat/notation-poc` at `6357e35` (PR 773). Read only: no source file was changed.
**For:** the maintainer. He found that a line could say only `in: :control`, a limit an agent chose. He asked for every other mistake of that kind, for a list of where the notation holds a branch, and for an account of the view machinery.

## Summary

Ranked by how wrong each finding is.

1. **Layers.** The notation takes one layer, `:control` (`primitive.rs:46`). The refusal says "Dialog-DB has no layers yet" (`expand.rs:679`). That is false today: the engine has `memory:`, `session:`, `control:`, channels and declared layers (`placement.rs:98-104`, `516-545`). The limit was the agent's (journal 2026-11-18), against the maintainer's word of 2026-11-16. Even `:control` is not the engine's `control:` layer: it compiles to a transient concept.
2. **Branch, not stack.** Every read goes through `Branch`: `Live`, `apply::answer`, `apply::rows`. So none of them sees `session:`, a declared memory layer or a durable layer. A document can be staged through a `StackTransaction`, but its answers are read back from the branch, so it cannot read what it just wrote to a layer.
3. **Definitions live in memory.** The analyzer keeps definitions in memory (`analyze.rs:9-11`, `expand.rs:2593-2657`). On 2026-11-20 the maintainer decided that "a mirror held in memory is not where names are resolved".
4. **Queries the engine could answer are answered here.** `select.rs:15` says "Dialog-DB cannot state a fold in a query". The engine has `Fold` and `subscribe_fold`. About 520 lines answer queries in memory, and a standing selection re-runs its whole answer whenever one row moves.
5. **The notation lets through less than the engine holds.** It takes one fold where the engine has six, 13 formulas where the engine has about 25 more, and 64-bit whole numbers where the engine holds 128-bit ones. None of these was the maintainer's decision.
6. **`Live` lays its own rules on `Branch::overlay`.** The engine's stack documentation calls the overlay the embedder's. About 245 lines keep reference counts there as facts, behind a process-wide lock. They exist only because `Branch::subscribe` takes no rule. That gap belongs in the engine.
7. **Rendering lives in the notation.** The keyed instance tree, the patches and the HTML allow-lists (about 1,700 lines without tests) do the renderer's work. tonk already writes its own serializer.
8. **"Unsupported" is used for two different things.** Its doc says "no counterpart in Dialog-DB yet" (`lib.rs:371-373`), but about 15 of its sites are design questions nobody decided. A reader cannot tell an engine limit from an agent's choice.
9. **tonk was not read.** I was not allowed to attach tonk-labs/tonk, so the comparison in Part 2 rests only on what this repository's notes cite of tonk.

## How this was done, and what was not checked

- I listed the limits by reading the source and by searching for every error constructor, `Unsupported`, `Refused` and `Reserved` site, `const` table, and string match. Two passes split the crate in halves, and I re-read the findings ranked above against the code.
- Origins come from `design/macros/journal.md`, `claims.md`, `gaps.md`, the other `design-*.md` notes, and PR 728's `research/notation/journal.md` (branch `docs/notation`).
- The branch is squashed, so `git log -S` dates nothing. An item marked "no record" means I searched those files for its wording and its subject and found nothing. It does not mean no conversation took place.
- "Agent" covers the agent, the coordinator and the managing agent alike. A journal entry that reads "**Decided.** By the agent" counts as (b), even when it rests on a decision of the maintainer's nearby.
- Not checked:
  - whether `Stack::subscribe`'s query hash stays stable for the per-entity queries a view lays;
  - whether a rule's conclusion may land in a memory layer (the engine routes rule heads to a channel, `placement.rs:49-53`; I did not test memory layers);
  - whether dialog-query accepts a query that binds no variable (Q17);
  - the claims at `live.rs:630-637` and `apply.rs:204-205`.
- tonk's source: see Part 2.

### Where reading the branch instead of the stack misses layers today

- **select** (`Live::select`, `live.rs:388-447`): reads `branch.query()`. It misses `session:`, declared memory layers and durable layers. The stack's `query()` (`stack.rs:796-809`) adds them.
- **queries in a document** (`apply::rows`, `apply.rs:449-470`): the same.
- **standing selects and views** (`Live::standing`, `Live::watch`): `Branch::subscribe` is `Subscription::over(branch, None, query)` (`subscription.rs:430-432`). It sees the primary and `Branch::overlay`. It misses everything above.
  - The stack has `Stack::subscription` (`subscription.rs:451`), `Stack::fold_subscription` (`fold.rs:179`), and a shared `Stack::subscribe` with `settle` (`stack/subscribe.rs:317`, `411`).
  - A write made through the stack into a memory layer never reaches a view. A write into the primary becomes visible only once it is published.
- **commands and check**: commands compile to a transient concept, not to `control:` (F2). Check reads nothing. Its only engine knowledge is the copied tables in Part 1.
- **apply**: `stage` can go through a `StackTransaction`, but `apply` takes a `Branch` and `answer` reads the branch. A document that writes into `session:` and then asks cannot see its own write.
- **the overlay itself**: `Live` writes its rules and its holder marks into `Branch::overlay`. The stack's doc says that overlay "is the embedder's, the stack writes nothing into it", and a stack read binds its rows to `memory:` (`stack.rs:107-112`). So the notation's bookkeeping facts show up as rows of the primary to any stack reader.

### The smallest traits that would replace the branch

Four. Each names what the crate does with the branch today, and nothing more:

1. **`Resolve`**: the definitions in force for a name, at one state. It replaces the in-memory table, and it is what the maintainer's "the analyzer is a resolver" asks for. It is the only one the analyzer needs.
2. **`Read`**: rows of a `ConceptQuery`, or a `Fold`, at the current state, with an optional `DeductiveRule` laid for that read. Both `Branch::query()` and `Stack::query()` return a `QueryLayer` that does this already.
3. **`Watch`**: subscribe to the same with its rule, and deliver `Delta`s. This needs the engine to take a rule beside a subscription. With that, the overlay lease goes.
4. **`Stage`**: the trait in `apply.rs:133` as it is, unsealed, with commit left to the caller.

`Stack` would implement all four. `Branch` would implement them for tests. The crate would then hold no `Branch`. The `Provider` bounds would move behind the implementations.

## Part 3. What a view is, and the machinery around it

### What a view compiles to

At the bottom, a view is rules:

- `(query HEAD conditions… (view ?x TEMPLATE))` with `(select HEAD)` compiles to `Step::View(Plan)` (`compile.rs:768-791`).
- The plan cuts the template into **layers**: one for the conditions every answer needs, and one for each collected read.
- For each layer, `Plan::query` (`view/plan.rs:1133-1214`) builds `(rule (view.layer SUBJECT row: {c0 ?a …}) premises…)` and compiles it to one `DeductiveRule` and one `ConceptQuery` of its head.
- A count on the root layer becomes a `Fold` (`plan.rs:959-1005`).

The engine sees ordinary rules, concept queries and folds. It keeps each one current with deltas and incremental maintenance: a demand cover, then per-entity re-derivation in DRed style, then a full recompute as the fallback (`subscription.rs:1-63`). It also refolds only the groups that moved (`fold.rs`).

What the engine does not do:
- it does not join the layers back into one answer;
- it does not turn rows into markup.

Those two, plus the plumbing to lay the rules, are the extra code.

### One view, from text to patch

From `tests/live.rs:679-683`; `it_loses_nothing_of_a_view_when_a_poll_fails` (`tests/live.rs:216`) drives the same path through `poll`.

```clj
(concept (list ?l name: ?n) (xyz.list/name of: ?l is: ?n as: :text cardinality: :one))
(rule (list/label ?l ?n) (list ?l name: ?n))
(query (list/chip ?l is: ?chip) (list/label ?l ?n) (view ?chip [:span ?n]))
(select (list/chip ?l is: ?chip))
```

1. **Read.** `parse` (`parse.rs:15`).
2. **Analyze.**
   - `Analyzer::analyze` (`analyze.rs:87`), then in `expand/query.rs`:
   - `define` (:252) keeps the query;
   - `sort` (:338; the `view` arm :357-377) checks the template with `view::template`;
   - `safe` (:445) refuses unbound template variables;
   - `events` (:523);
   - `selected` (:588) builds the select;
   - `reach` (:625; `view` :777-782).
3. **Compile.**
   - `compile` (`compile.rs:603`) → `compiled` (:768) → `is_markup` (`plan.rs:415`) → `Plan::of` (`plan.rs:830`);
   - inside `Plan::of`: `Defined::read` (:354), `markup::template` (:619), `join` (:1009), `rows` (:1067);
   - each layer is test-compiled through `plan.query` (`compile.rs:780`). The result is `Step::View`.
4. **Watch.**
   - `Live::watch` (`live.rs:524`) → `Maintainer::new` (`tree.rs:271`) → `plan.folds()` → `plan.query` (`live.rs:545`);
   - → `lay` (`live.rs:276`): `overlay.assert(rule)` at :331, plus LAID and HOLDER marks;
   - → `branch.subscribe(query.select)` (`live.rs:547`).
5. **Poll.**
   - `Watched::poll` (`live.rs:853`) → `Subscription::poll` (`subscription.rs:767`) gives `Delta { asserted, retracted }`;
   - → `kept` and `merged` (`live.rs:575-600`) → `Plan::row` (`plan.rs:1237`) turns each conclusion into a `Row`, collected into a `Change` (`tree.rs:151`);
   - → `Maintainer::apply_counted` (`tree.rs:698`) → `Vec<Patch>`: Insert, Remove, SetText, SetAttribute, SetEvent (`tree.rs:103`). The first poll inserts each root (`live.rs:913-924`).
6. **Asked once instead.** `apply::answer` → `Live::rendered` (`live.rs:461`) reads each layer with `branch.query().with(rule)` and feeds `tree::render` (`tree.rs:948`).

**Judgement.**
- What is the notation's own work: reading the template, compiling it to rules, and saying how instances are keyed.
- What belongs elsewhere:
  - in the engine: the overlay lease, the polling of many subscriptions, the row mirror, and the in-memory select;
  - in the renderer: the tree, the patches and the HTML checks.
- Moved, roughly 2,000 of the 4,200 lines that are not tests would go.
- The cost record agrees that the extra is the notation's, not the engine's. At ten thousand items, a rename costs 25 ms against 0.95 ms for a plain subscription: "the cost is the notation's own" (journal 2026-11-24).

## One-line remedies

- F1, F2: take every layer the stack declares in `in:`; compile `:control` to `control:`.
- Part 2: hold a `Stack` behind the four traits above, never a `Branch`.
- F9: resolve definitions through `Resolve`, against the stack.
- F4-F7: let through what the engine has: its folds, formulas and 128-bit numbers.
- `Unsupported`: split it into an engine limit and an undecided choice, so the message says which.
- Part 3: ask the engine for a subscription that takes its rule; hand keyed rows to the renderer.
