## Why it works

The readers who scored worked the expansion through themselves:

- "the `memo` rule expands per attribute, so it derives `xyz.tonk.post content:` for `mail:one` from the email body. The `note` concept reads that same `xyz.tonk.post content:` attribute";
- "A's expansion gives `xyz.todo issue:7 title` a derived value "Fix login". So B finds `?old` = "Fix login" and fires."

Every earlier attempt described the model in prose:

- "a domain call reads derived values";
- "a concept is an open-ended concept";
- "a concept is a bidirectional lens";
- "a rule concludes each attribute".

This one shows a rule's conclusion rewritten into the very domain form readers had taken for storage. After that there is no separate derived layer to keep it in. A conclusion is an `(xyz.acme.task ?t title: ?title)` head, the same shape a condition reads.

The misses, 1 of 20 and 4 of 20, kept the old reason: B needs "a stored `xyz.todo` title for `issue:7`".

## What remains

**How `last` elects.** With the rename landing, 9 of the 16 said the to-do now has two titles and that nothing says which wins. 7 guessed "Fix login bug". On question 9, readers split again between the stored title and the newer derived one. R29's sentence on `last` is still needed beside the expansion.

## What follows

- The expansion belongs in the spec, as the maintainer proposed, with the sentence on `last` beside it.
- The maintainer suggested a simpler example. The test used a `task` concept with two fields, which already worked. A one-field example is worth trying once the `last` sentence is added, to see whether it loses anything.
