## Not in the rubric

**How readers marked the to-do done (item 5).** All fifteen created it through `add!`, and none created a second to-do. Ten read its entity back with a `query` and wrote `(xyz.todo/state! todo:<id> is: :done)` in a later document; five defined a `done` or `finish` command whose rule recomputes the entity with `id`. Most said why: a top-level fact must be ground, and `id` is a formula only a rule can use, so a document cannot name an entity a command derived without asking for it first.

**What readers named as most confusing.** `!` still, but now for one reason: `add!` and `todo!` are spelled alike, while one lasts one tick and the other is stored. Eleven of fifteen proposed marking modifiers apart from concepts:

| proposal | readers |
|---|---|
| a sigil on the modifier: `@add`, `&add` | 6 |
| a word around it: `(hold add …)`, `(once add …)`, `(later add …)`, `(modifier add …)` | 5 |
| a word for committing: `(commit …)` | 3 |
| a word for each meaning: `(store …)`, `(signal …)` | 1 |

One reader also named the retract that reaches a concept sharing an attribute as a trap.
