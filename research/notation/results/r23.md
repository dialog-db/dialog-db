# R23: `!` as induction, with the commit said by the document or by the mode

2026-11-10 (UTC). 10 readers, 2 documents, model `haiku`, read-only agent, all started at once and interleaved. Scored by the rubric in [r23-plan](r23-plan.md); every answer, with its preference, is in [`r23.jsonl`](r23.jsonl). No reader called anything but Read.

## Tally

| document | scores (of 18) | median | clarity | names `!` most confusing | prefers `syntax` | prefers `mode` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `syntax` | 17, 18, 18, 18, 18 | 18 | all 3 | 5 | 2 | 3 |
| `mode` | 18, 18, 18, 18, 18 | 18 | all 3 | 1 | 0 | 5 |

The one miss: a `syntax` reader said a scoped `(assert (todo …))` is a command's tagged fact, so the same-tick query cannot see it.

## Against the bars

- **Reads right:** both medians 18 of 18. Yes.
- **Induction reads:** item 15, the `remind` that re-issues itself while the to-do is open, 10 of 10, every reader writing `(remind! (todo ?t))` with a premise that the to-do is open.
- **The preference:** 8 of 10 chose `mode`, past the bar of 7. It is not only each group choosing its own: three of five `syntax` readers chose the other.

## Why they chose

**For `mode`** (8): `!` keeps one meaning, timing, where in `syntax` it also turns a whole document into a transaction (6); the same document can be run as a query to check it and then as a transaction, without editing it (4); a stray `!` cannot turn a read into a write (2); a missing `!` cannot silently make a write a no-op (1).

**For `syntax`** (2): a rule should say in its text what it lands; under `mode`, whether a head's `(assert (todo …))` lands depends on how the document that set the rule off was submitted.

## Not in the rubric

**What readers named as most confusing.** In `syntax`, all five named `!` doing two jobs, the document's mode and induction, and four proposed a `(transaction …)` or `(query …)` wrapper: the mode, spelled in the document. In `mode`, one named `!`, asking for `(later …)` on the inductive head; the others named that a concept, a domain call and a command look alike (1), that a left-out parameter matches anything in a premise but is an error in a command (1), that the primary means different things by position (1), and that the mode is decided outside the document (1, proposing `(transact …)`).
