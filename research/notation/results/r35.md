# R35: leaving `message` and `audience` undefined changes nothing

2026-11-13 (UTC). 40 readers on the clean runner (`claude-haiku-5-5`), single turn. Scored by the rubric in [r35-plan](r35-plan.md); every answer is in [`r35.jsonl`](r35.jsonl).

| variant | the query finds the memo as a note |
|---|---|

| `undef-derive` | 11 of 20 |

For comparison, on the derive case with `message` and `audience` defined:

Against the bars, `undef-assert` works and `undef-derive` lands between them.

Predictions:

- **`undef-assert`, held.** I expected it near the ceiling, and all 20 found the memo.
- **`undef-derive`, held, but only at the low end.** I expected 10 to 16, and it scored 11.

It did not rise above the defined versions, so the definitions do not pull readers back to a stored layer.

## What it shows

Defining `message` and `audience` neither helps nor hurts. With no definition, readers took `message` for a stored attribute itself: every `undef-assert` reader stored `test:one message "hello world"`. The derive case still split about in half, as it did with the definitions.

What helps is that `note` and `memo` share a name below them, whatever that name is. When `memo` and `note` read `xyz.tonk.post` directly, no reader found the memo. When both go through a shared `message`, about half do: 25 of 50 over R33 to R35.

The other half misses for R34's reason. Readers take a rule as reached only through its head's name: "the `memo` rule only derives `memo`, and it isn't triggered by this query", or "it fires only when `memo` is involved". Several described the forward reading and then set it aside: "if the engine also applies rules forward when querying, the `memo` rule would derive `message is: "hello world"` for `mail:one`... I can't tell which".

## What follows

As in R34: what is left is the evaluation model, and the test is to state it. One sentence, on the derive case: a rule's conclusion gives values to the attributes behind its concept whether or not anything asks for that concept, and every read of those attributes sees them. Run it on the plain domain form and on the shared-name form, 20 readers each.
