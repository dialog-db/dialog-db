## Item 5: the undeclared concept

The design says `(W (project ?p name: ?n))` with no `project` concept is a compile error. Every reader said "empty or an error", and the point went to those who leaned to the error.

| leaning | readers |
|---|---|
| empty result | 10 |
| undecided | 6 |
| error | 4 |

This is the same for every word, so it is a finding about the design, not the word: a reader's default for an unknown name is an empty relation, as in Datalog. Item 6 shows readers did draw the line the design means, that a domain call like `xyz.todo` needs no declaration where a concept does; they only did not expect the undeclared concept to be refused.

Scoring note: the plan gives item 5 to "an error". Since every answer hedged, a hedge earned the point only when it leaned to the error ("most likely an error, or at best empty"). Counting every hedge would give every reader 7 and change nothing between words.

## What readers found hardest

19 of 20 named `(id ?t list: ?l text: ?x)`: whether it mints a fresh entity, derives one from the fields, or looks one up. The twentieth named how the rule's `todo` head reaches the `xyz.todo` facts the concept reads, which two others also raised.

Unscored, but worth the design's attention: all 20 said `add` itself writes a to-do (one said it "looks like a write"), and the rule then fills in the state. The design has `add` write nothing that is kept; only the rule's assert lands. The samples gave no way to tell, so this is the reader's default, not a misreading of a statement.
