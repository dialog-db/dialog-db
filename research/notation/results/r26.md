# R26: how a type constraint is spelled, read cold

2026-11-10 (UTC). 25 readers, 5 documents, model `haiku`, read-only agent. Scored by the rubric in [r26-plan](r26-plan.md); every answer is in [`r26.jsonl`](r26.jsonl). No reader called anything but Read.

Two departures from the plan. The session runs at most 20 readers at once, so readers 1 to 4 of each variant started together and reader 5 of each started about two minutes later. Three readers (two `colon`, one `var`) took the server instructions appended after the file for the whole Read result and answered nothing; their transcripts show the file was returned. They were replaced, with a prompt that adds one sentence saying the file's content comes first. Their ids are in the scratchpad's `failed.txt`.

## The predictions

Both failed. No `keyword` reader read `(:text ?x)` as reading a `text` field of `?x`; all five called it a check or a guard on a value. No `name` reader said `(text ?x)` reads stored data; all five called it a check. With an `xyz.todo` line beside them, readers told the read from the constraints in every spelling.

## What readers found hardest

22 of 25 named the variable label in `(xyz.notebook ?n ?p: ?b)`. All 25 still read it as every field of `?n` in the domain, its name as `?p`, but most said they were guessing. About half (12 of 25) were unsure whether a constraint is checked on a read, on an assert, or both, and hedged question 4 between refusing the write and storing it but hiding it from queries. Both have to be stated where the notation is taught.

## What this round cannot say

The questions name the three choices, read, write or neither, so they may have made the right answer easy to find. A round that asks readers to write a concept from a description, without showing the constraint form, would measure which spelling agents produce, which is the question this one could not reach.
