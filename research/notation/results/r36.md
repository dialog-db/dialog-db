## What it shows

**The sentence and the shared names are substitutes, not complements.** Either one gets the domain case from none to about 12 in 20. Both together get no further. Each answers the same question: how a rule's `memo` reaches `note`.

- The shared name answers it by making `note` and `memo` visibly read the same thing.
- The sentence answers it by saying a rule's conclusion reaches the attributes.

The readers who scored in `domain-sentence` cited it: "per the one-sentence rule, [the memo] concludes the `xyz.tonk.post` content for `mail:one`".

**The remaining misses ask two different questions,** neither of which the sentence answers:

1. **Is `note` matched by its attributes or by name?** "The one documented propagation goes from a concept to its attributes, not to a different concept such as `note`. The main uncertainty is whether concept membership is inferred from attributes." Nominal against structural matching.
2. **When does a rule run?** "The `memo` rule would produce one, but only if rules run forward on assert, which the documentation doesn't say." Several readers in both variants left the answer to an engine they could not see.

The domain misses were mostly the first: "the rule concludes `memo`, not `note`, so nothing connects the stored email to a note".

## What follows

Both open questions have one-sentence answers, and readers asked for the first in R32's second turn (17 of 20 derive readers):

- **Structural matching:** a concept matches any entity that has its attributes, whoever gave them values.
- **Eager rules:** a rule's conclusion holds as soon as its conditions do, whether or not anything asks for its concept.

The next round adds them to the maintainer's sentence, on the domain form, where the notation would stay as it is.
