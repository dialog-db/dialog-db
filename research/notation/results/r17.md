## Not in the rubric

**Every reader rated the notation 3 of 5, and 9 of 10 named the same thing as most confusing: `!`.** They count three readings of one mark: a write that lands in the next tick, a stored read in a premise (`(bug! …)` against `(bug …)`), and a rule head that commits only when its trigger came from a `!`. The tenth named content identity: the same `assert!` with another state silently makes another to-do.

What they proposed:

| proposal | readers |
|---|---|
| name the stored read in a premise: `(stored (bug …))` or `(stored bug …)` | 5 |
| name the next tick: `(next (add …))`, `next: true`, `add-next` | 6 |
| a named mutation verb such as `(store (todo …))` | 1 |
| write the trigger in the premise: `(add! …)` for "fires only on a stored add" | 1 |
| make the identity's fields visible: `key: [list text]` | 1 |

Readers who proposed both keep `!` for writes only.

**A gap in the document.** Three readers said task 5 rests on something the card does not say: whether an uncommitted `!` head counts as a deduction a same-tick query can see. Two answered no, one yes.
