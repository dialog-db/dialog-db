## What it shows

**The table was not the anchor.** The `prose` readers had no table and no attribute names in the facts, and missed exactly as before. They gave the same reason: B needs "a stored `xyz.todo` title for issue:7". They took the stored layer from rule B's own spelling.

**The spelling was the anchor.** Every `concept` reader said B reads the title rule A derives, so B fires, C finds nothing stored to retract, and D stores "Fix login bug". Readers have always taken a concept call as stored and derived; R30 confirms they apply that to a condition in a rule too. They take a domain call as the stored layer, and no documentation tried in R28 or R29 moved them reliably.

**The election still has to be said.** The `concept` readers all guessed that "Fix login bug" shows, and nearly all hedged that the notation does not say how `last` orders a stored value against a derived one. On question 9 they split: 4 said "Login fails" by recency, and 6 said the stored "Fix login bug" still wins. In R29, the sentence on `last` took every reader who reached that point to "Login fails". So that sentence is still needed. The one on domain calls is not, once rules read through concepts.

**What readers found hardest.** As in every round: telling a document's request (`rename`, `archive`) from a stored or derived fact. The `concept` readers added the stored-against-derived question for `last`. One reader in `prose` named the misreading exactly as a rule: "a condition that names a stored attribute sees only stored facts and never a derived answer".

## What follows

Two readings, for the maintainer to choose between:

- **Rules read through concepts.** Domain calls stay legal and keep dialog's meaning, but the documentation and examples write rule conditions as concept calls. Readers then expect what dialog does. The cost is that a rule which needs one attribute of a domain needs a concept for it.
- **Domain calls change spelling.** If a domain call is to be read the way dialog reads it, it should not look like the stored layer. That needs a spelling to test, and it is the harder change.

Whichever reading is chosen, the sentence on how `last` elects between stored and derived values belongs in the documentation.
