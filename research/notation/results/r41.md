## What it shows

**Writing a conclusion as a claim carries most of the model.** On the to-do, the one-field example adds 6 readers (14 to 20). Without it, the misses kept R39's reason: B "needs a stored `xyz.todo/title` claim". So the example stays, but it is no longer what carries the to-do.

**A pick on a line names its claims.** Every `list` reader said each list keeps its newest task, and none gave a to-do one list. R40's `lines` had 17 of 20 the other way.

**The note slipped, on nominal matching.** All 7 misses said the rule "concludes `memo`, not `note`". Several called a concept "a view" whose lines "describe what it reads". The new paragraph introduces a line as something that "reads the claims of one attribute", and says nothing of a concluded concept concluding its lines; only the example shows that. In R39 the lines had no explanation of their own.

**Readers expect a conclusion to live where its facts do.** The design reads a location as a set of facts that rules run over, and a condition naming its own `in:` reads that place whatever the read names. So in `place-fixed`, where `draft` fixes `in: :memory`, a replica read shows the draft's note. 14 of 20 said it does not: "the rule's conclusion comes from `draft:1`, whose claim is kept at `:memory`". They place a conclusion with its facts.

**Half have no place for a conclusion at all.** On both `place` documents, about half said a memory read shows nothing: "the rule head has no `at:`, so the code names no place where that claim is kept". A conclusion with no place is invisible to every read that names one, and that undoes R39 for any concept taking `at:`.

## What follows

- The line paragraph should say a line is a claim, read when the concept is asked for and concluded when it is concluded, rather than only "reads". To test on `note`.
- `in:` needs a sentence on conclusions. Readers' default is that a concluded claim is kept where the facts it was concluded from are kept, beside "as recent as the newest fact it was concluded from". That is a design choice the design has not made: it differs from "rules run over the read's place" when a condition fixes its own `in:`, and it needs a rule for facts from two places.
