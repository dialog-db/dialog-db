## What it shows

**`cardinality: :one` carries the election as `pick: :last` did.** With R39's sentence, now "a read gives the one caused most recently, whether it was stored or concluded by a rule", readers kept stored and concluded values in one election.

**Without a sentence, the ranking in a conclusion is invisible.** No `account-bare` reader mentioned the list. They read `status` as `:one` over concluded values and gave the newest: after `:active` was asserted again, all 20 said `:active`. One sentence ("keeps the one listed first; a value not listed ranks last") took that to 20 of 20, for the right reason: "The ranking depends on list position, not on how many claims or how recent".

**Readers accept writing the ranked field.** Every reader took `(assert (account acct:1 status: :active))` as a stored `account/status` claim that wins by recency over the value the fold chose. That is what the cardinality work does.

## What follows

- The spec's line paragraph takes `cardinality:` in place of `pick:`.
- A ranking in a conclusion needs its sentence in the spec, and probably a spelling that names it: a bare list is read as a type elsewhere (`as: [:open :done]`).
- Not asked: the override in question 4 stays newer than the fold's choice until a newer top-ranked state arrives. A later `:registered` state changes nothing. Worth a question of its own.
