# R40: a policy on a variable line reads as the concept field's policy

2026-11-13 (UTC). 40 readers on the clean runner (`claude-haiku-5-5`), single turn. Scored by hand by the rubric in [r40-plan](r40-plan.md); every answer is in [`r40.jsonl`](r40.jsonl).

Predictions:

- **`inline` between, held**, at the top of the range I gave (8 to 14).

## What it shows

**On its own line, a policy is read as the policy of the concept's field.** 17 of 20 `lines` readers gave the to-do a single list:

- "the `list` attribute of `todo:a` elects a single claim under `:last`";
- "It makes `list` single-valued with last-write-wins".

They read `list:` as an attribute of the to-do, whose claims are the `xyz.list` facts pointing at it. The documentation says a policy elects among the claims of one attribute of one entity, and these readers found the entity in the concept's head rather than in the call. The 3 who scored did the opposite: "`?l` is never the value of an attribute in this concept".

**In place, it mostly reads as where it sits.** 13 of 20 `inline` readers saw the policy in the entity position and said it has no claims to elect: "`?l` is the subject of the `task:` attribute, not a value read from an attribute". The 7 who did not split two ways:

- 3 still gave the to-do one list;
- 4 applied it to the call's attribute, `list:home`'s `task` claims, so each list keeps only its newest task. That is not dialog's reading, but it is a reading of the claims the call reads, not of the concept's field.

**The draft states something dialog cannot do.** "A to-do has one list" cannot be said with a policy when the list holds the link: the claims belong to the lists, one cell per list. It needs the link stored on the to-do, `(xyz.todo ?i list: ?l)` under `:last`, or a rule. Most `lines` readers took the draft to mean the thing it cannot do. Nothing in either spelling warns the author.

## What follows

- A policy on a line of its own does not say which claims it elects, and readers fill in the concept's field. Where a variable appears only as an entity, or in two calls, that is the wrong cell.
- In place, the constraint sits on the claims it elects, and readers mostly see that. Wrapping a variable is what the maintainer finds hard to read, so the choice is between that and a line that misleads.
- A policy on a variable that binds no value should be an error, as design.md already says, and the error is what teaches the author.
- `in:` was left out and needs its own round, in whichever place a policy goes.
