## What it shows

**`/?n` needs no explanation.** Every reader, labeled or not, read `xyz.tonk.element.method/?n` as "the method's name, spliced into the attribute name", gave both methods, and kept entries at different positions apart after a sync: "N2 and N3 are different attributes, so nothing conflicts".

**Without a label, a sequence reads as a dictionary of ids.** 18 of 20 `none` readers said both concepts are keyed the same way, a notebook's `N1` being "an opaque entry id", and several argued against the `at:` field: "The `at:` label suggests a position, but the key is an identifier, not an index".

**Any of the labels fixes it.** The values carry it as much as the word: `shape:`'s three misses called `N1` "an entry id" despite `:sequence`.

## What follows

The collection line is `(domain/?key of: ?e is: ?v as: … <label>: …)` with a label for the kind of key. `keyed:` and `by:` both scored 20 of 20; the choice between them is the maintainer's.
