## What the sentence reached and what it did not

Readers did use the sentence. Several quoted it in question 3: the query returns `issue:7` "since the domain call reads every value, stored or derived". But question 3 is a concept query, which all 20 R27 readers answered correctly with no sentence at all, so quoting it there cost nothing.

In question 8 the same readers went back to stored facts. Eight of the ten `stated` readers wrote some form of "B needs a stored `xyz.todo` title for issue:7, and issue:7 has only `xyz.tracker` facts". They checked the stored list in the prompt, not the sentence.

The step they skip is not the one the sentence states. To see that rule B fires, a reader has to join two things:

- rule A concludes a `todo`;
- the `todo` concept maps `title` to `xyz.todo/title`.

Only then is rule A's title a value of `xyz.todo/title`. The sentence says a domain call reads derived values. It never says that a concept's conclusion is a derived value of the domain fields behind it. Both readers who scored took that step themselves: "B reads the to-do's current title, which for issue:7 is the derived Fix login" (`stated` 6).

**Question 9.** `stated` 6 said "Login fails" for dialog's reason: the stored "Fix login bug" is older than the issue's change, so the derived title wins. `stated` 2 kept "Fix login bug". It reasoned that nothing retracted the stored title, and it said it could not tell how `last` orders a stored value against a derived one. Every other reader said "Login fails" because, on their reading, the rename stored nothing.

**What readers found hardest.** As in R27: telling a document's request (`rename`, `archive`) apart from a stored or derived fact. The two readers who scored question 8 both named the next gap themselves, how `last` elects between a stored value and a derived one. They were right that nothing they read says so.

## What follows

A sentence about domain calls is the wrong sentence. The candidate to test is the step readers skipped: a rule that concludes a concept derives values of that concept's domain fields, and a pick elects among stored and derived values by when they were last caused. Whether readers need that stated, or need to see a derived value flow through the concept in an example, is the question a further round can ask.
