# R33: one-attribute concepts carry a rule's conclusion to other concepts

2026-11-13 (UTC). 20 readers on the clean runner (`claude-haiku-5-5`), single turn. Scored by the rubric in [r33-plan](r33-plan.md); every answer is in [`r33.jsonl`](r33.jsonl).

## Tally

| variant | the query finds the memo as a note | names `xyz.tonk.post/content` (or `xyz.mail/body`) | says information flows both ways |
|---|---|---|---|
| `assert` | 10 of 10 | 9 | 10 |
| `derive` | 7 of 10 | 3 | 10 |
| R32 `assert-bare`, for comparison | 10 of 10 | 1 | 10 |
| R32 `derive-bare`, for comparison | 0 of 10 | 5 | 9 |

Against the bars, both variants work. `derive` reaches 7 of 10 exactly, where R32's otherwise identical code scored 0. The only change from R32 is that `note` and `memo` read `xyz.tonk.post` through one-attribute concepts, `message` and `audience`. My prediction for `derive` was between the bars; it reached the bar.

Ten readers is a small sample, and 7 is the threshold, so this wants a second run before it is relied on.

## Why it worked

The readers who found the note reasoned the same way. In one reader's words, "`note` expands to `message`, which expands to `xyz.tonk.post ... content:`". The rule says a `memo` holds for `mail:one`, and `memo` "expands to a `message` (a `post` with content) plus an `audience`", so `note` reads that content.

In R32, `memo` expanded straight into an `xyz.tonk.post` form, and readers would not let a derived memo produce domain facts. Here `memo` expands into `message`, a named concept that `note` also uses. Readers chain through named concepts freely, as R30 found when rule B read `(todo ...)`. They do not chain through a domain form, which they take for storage. One-attribute concepts give `note` and `memo` a shared named concept, and the conclusion follows the chain.

The three who missed it kept the rule's conclusion to its own head, as R32's readers did: "the `memo` rule derives `memo` for `mail:one`, but `note` is not `memo`... This assumes rules only fire from body to head."

Two readers who scored added that it depends on the engine applying rules at query time.

## A side effect on the assert case

With `message` and `audience` in between, 9 of 10 `assert` readers named the stored claims `xyz.tonk.post/content` and `xyz.tonk.post/audience`, against 1 of 10 in R32. Putting one attribute in each concept made the attribute readable as an attribute. In the `derive` variant, `email` still reads `xyz.mail` directly, and most of those readers again took `xyz.mail` for a type with fields.

## What follows

- **Replicate `derive`** with ten more readers, since 7 sits on the bar.
- **Test the maintainer's next two spellings** against it, to find which part does the work:
  - `message` and `audience` spelled as attributes rather than concepts. If readers then read them as storage, `derive` should fall back toward 0.
  - `note` and `memo` spelled as records or structs.
