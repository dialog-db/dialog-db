Scored by the rubric in [r27-plan](r27-plan.md); every answer is in [`r27.jsonl`](r27.jsonl). No reader called anything but Read. ## The written rules

All 20 wrote the archive as two rules, one retracting `xyz.note/text` and one asserting `xyz.archive/text`, each reading the text in its conditions. The ten `bare` readers wrote no `when`; the ten `when` readers kept it. The prediction that some `bare` readers would put two conclusions in one rule failed: none did. Several said why: "a rule seems to have one conclusion, so the retract and the assert each need their own rule".

## Reported, not scored

**When the retract and assert take effect** (item 6). 16 of 20 said later, after the step that derived them, the same 8 in each variant; 4 said in the same step. Those who said later mostly reasoned that the rules read the store as it was, so the writes apply after; one `when` reader added that a retract applied first would stop the assert's condition from matching. Nothing in the code said so, and most got it anyway.

**Renaming the derived to-do** (item 8). All 20 said the rename does nothing: rule B reads the old title with `(xyz.todo ?t title: ?old)`, and they read that as stored facts only, of which `issue:7` has none. So no update, the to-do still shows "Fix login", the issue too. That is not what dialog does. A rule's conclusion derives into its concept's attributes, so rule A's to-do title is under `xyz.todo/title` too, and a domain call reads it, elected with the stored claims. Rule B does fire, with old "Fix login"; the retract finds nothing stored; the assert stores "Fix login bug", which is newer than the issue fact the derived title stands by, so the to-do shows "Fix login bug" and the issue keeps "Fix login". That is the maintainer's expectation, and every reader expected the opposite: readers take a domain call for the stored layer and a concept for stored plus derived. The design has to say that a domain call reads derived facts too.

**A later change to the issue's title** (item 9). All 20 said "Login fails". By dialog's code that is right whatever the rename did: the derived title stands by the newer issue fact and wins.

## What readers found hardest

Almost every reader named the same thing in one form or another: telling apart what a form is, a request in the document (`rename`, `archive`), a stored fact, a derived one, or a write. Nothing in the notation marks the difference. One reader named exactly the item 8 misreading, whether a pattern "reads stored facts or derived conclusions", and another that a rename matching nothing fails silently. One noticed that a `rename` left in the document would fire again on the next evaluation, with old and new titles equal.
