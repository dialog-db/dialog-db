# R32: readers take a concept for a two-way lens, but not a rule's conclusion

2026-11-13 (UTC). 40 readers on the clean runner (`claude -p`, `claude-haiku-5-5`), each a session of two turns. Scored by the rubric in [r32-plan](r32-plan.md); both turns of every reader are in [`r32.jsonl`](r32.jsonl). Every session completed both turns.

## Tally

| variant | the query finds the memo as a note | says information flows both ways | names `xyz.tonk.post/content` as the attribute |
|---|---|---|---|
| `assert-bare` | 10 of 10 | 10 | 1 |
| `assert-spec` | 10 of 10 | 10 | 10 |
| `derive-bare` | 0 of 10 | 9 | 5 |
| `derive-spec` | 0 of 10 | 10 | 10 |

The question 2 point goes to `test:one` with "hello world", or `mail:one` in the derive variants. The last column counts the stored or derived facts in question 1 that name attributes by their full name, `xyz.mail/body` included.

Against the bars, `assert-bare` and `assert-spec` work, and `derive-bare` and `derive-spec` are no help. All four predictions held except `derive-spec`, which I expected between the bars and which scored 0.

## The hypothesis, answered

The maintainer's hypothesis was that models read a concept as a one-way, Datalog-style view. They do not, at least not when asked: 39 of 40 said information flows both ways between `memo` and `xyz.tonk.post`. Asserting a memo writes its fields, and querying a note reads them. Every reader who asserted a memo found it as a note, with or without documentation.

What readers will not grant is that a **rule's conclusion** goes through the lens. With a rule concluding `memo`, all 20 said the note query is empty, because "the only stored facts are `xyz.mail` facts" and the rule's memo exists only when `memo` is queried. The paraphrase of the anatomy did not move this at all. It says a concept is a bidirectional lens where querying composes and *asserting* decomposes. Readers applied that faithfully to asserts and not to rules, since it says nothing about rules. The essay says nothing about them in this sense either: it calls deductive rules unidirectional unless the mapping is non-lossy.

So the misreading of R27 to R31 was never about concepts. It is about what a rule's conclusion is: readers take it for a query-time view, Datalog's derived layer, never for values of the attributes it names.

## A second finding: the domain form reads as a record

Without documentation, 9 of the 10 `assert-bare` readers never named `xyz.tonk.post/content`. They read `(xyz.tonk.post ?memo content: ...)` as a record of type `xyz.tonk.post` with a field `content`. At least three listed a separate stored fact saying `test:one` is an `xyz.tonk.post`. With the anatomy paraphrase, which names `xyz.tonk.post/content` as an attribute, all 10 listed exactly the two claims.

## What readers said would have made it obvious

In turn 2 every reader was told dialog's answer and asked what would have made it obvious. 2 of the 20 derive readers and most assert readers had expected it in part. The suggestions overlap; counts are of readers who made each.

| suggestion | `assert` (20) | `derive` (20) |
|---|---|---|
| write the full attribute name in the concept, e.g. `(?note xyz.tonk.post/content ...)` or `(xyz.tonk.post/content ?note ?message)` | 16 | 15 |
| say that attributes are global and a concept matches by attribute, whoever wrote them | 6 | 17 |
| say that a rule's conclusion becomes claims, as asserting the concept would | 0 | 14 |
| show a worked example | 16 | 12 |
| declare attributes once, e.g. `(attribute xyz.tonk.post/content :text)` | 9 | 5 |

The turn-2 text itself named attributes and a rule's conclusion, so these suggestions partly echo it. The notation suggestions are the readers' own.

## What follows

- **The doc fix is one sentence about rules.** For example: "a rule that concludes a concept gives values to its attributes, exactly as asserting the concept would". R29 tested nearly that sentence with the session's context and got 6 of 10. It has not been tested clean, and never beside the lens framing.
- **The notation fix the readers asked for is to write attributes by their full name.** The domain form hides the attribute behind a record shape, so `content:` looks local to `xyz.tonk.post`, and two concepts over it look unrelated. This is the spelling of a domain call that R31 said remained to be found.

The two can be tested together in one round: the lens paraphrase plus the rule sentence, with and without full attribute names, on the derive case.
