## What the context changed

Not the main result. The `clean` readers misread rule B's `(xyz.todo ...)` exactly as the `agent` readers did, and read `(todo ...)` correctly exactly as they did. Their reason was word for word the same: B needs "a stored `xyz.todo` title for `issue:7`". The spelling misleads models that have never seen dialog, its memory or its connectors.

Two differences are worth noting, with small counts behind them:

- **`subset` did better without the context:** 4 of 10 against 0 of 7. The readers who scored said the example shows rule conclusions appearing as `xyz.*` values, and carried that to rule B.
- **The `clean` readers would not guess the election.** On R30's `concept`, all ten `agent` readers had guessed that "Fix login bug" shows. Eight of the ten `clean` readers said instead that the to-do has two titles and nothing says which `last` picks. One also said a later issue change leaves both. These readers are more literal, so the gap the R29 sentence filled is wider here.

## What follows

- **Describing a domain call as a concept does not work.** Neither in a sentence nor through the subset example: readers still read `(xyz.todo ...)` as a lookup into storage. The `subset` example came closest, 4 of 10, and only on the clean runner.
- **Spelling a read as a concept call works everywhere.** It has now scored 20 of 20 across both runners, with no documentation.
- **The election has to be stated.** Without it, readers who get the rename right cannot say what the to-do shows.

The maintainer has ruled out changing the engine, and has said convention alone leaves the wrong model in place. So the remaining lever is the spelling of a domain call. The question for the next round is which spelling makes readers treat it as a concept: for example, the concept's own form with the domain as its name, or a marker that a call is open-ended rather than stored.

From now on, rounds run on the `clean` runner. Results from R17 to R30 stand as comparisons within their rounds; their levels came from readers who saw the session's context.
