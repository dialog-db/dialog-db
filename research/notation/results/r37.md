## Why they said no

These are counted by hand from the 16 who said no; a reader may give more than one reason.

- **12: the rule makes a `memo`, not a `note`.** "The `memo` rule derives memos from emails, not notes"; "nothing links `mail:one` to `note`". They match concepts by name: a `note` is something concluded or asserted as a note.
- **7: the rule never runs, since nothing asks for `memo`.** "The `memo` rule would produce a `message` fact for `mail:one`, but nothing in the query asks for `memo`, so the rule never runs."
- **4 described the other reading and set it aside.** "The answer would flip to yes only if the system forward-chains rules after each assert, which the code doesn't show."

## What it shows

The open question flattered the rule case. When readers can list a chain of possibilities, about half end on the memo. When they must commit, their default is the nominal and goal-directed model:

- a concept is its name;
- a rule fires when its head is asked for.

So the shared `message` name does not, by itself, give readers the structural model. It only gives them a path to reason along when invited to.

The two questions R36 left are the same two here, now the main reasons rather than the remainder:

1. Is a concept matched by its attributes or by its name?
2. Does a rule's conclusion hold without anyone asking for it?

## What follows

Use the direct question from now on; it measures the reader's committed model. Then test the sentences that answer those two questions, on this document and on the domain form:

- a concept matches any entity that has its attributes, whoever gave them values;
- a rule's conclusion holds as soon as its conditions do, whether or not anything asks for its concept.
