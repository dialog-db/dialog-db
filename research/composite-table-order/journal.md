# Journal: composite table order

## Where things stand

**Shelved.** Rejected against the bars written before the runs
([../composite-table-order.md](../composite-table-order.md)): records'
leaves fall 6.7 percent (bar 10), encoding is 10 to 15 percent slower (bar
5). The attribute ordering, where the hypothesis put the gain, fell 0.9
percent: the table header's `size` differs between most neighbours, so
front coding stops before the first atom under either layout. Nothing is
next unless the header itself is reconsidered, which is a different
question.

## Datasets

Synthetic, generated by the harness from the seed `0x5eed_c0de_7ab1_e0d3`
(`rust/dialog-baseline/examples/composite_table_order.rs`), never written
to the repository:

- records: 4,000 entities, one `app/profile` record each of `person/name`,
  `person/city` (texts from fixed lists), `person/age` (a natural),
  `person/active` (a boolean), `person/manager` (an entity); 566,138 bytes
  of composites. The skewed variant has the same city and flag on nine
  entities in ten (566,200 bytes).
- syntax trees, a stand-in: 2,000 random forms up to four deep in
  `census.py`'s node shape (524,846 bytes). The notation's own sources are
  in another repository the measuring session could not reach.
- mixed: 4,000 entities, each a record of one of three shapes (3, 5 or 7
  fields) under `app/item` and a list of 3 to 8 numbers and texts under
  `app/tags` (699,438 bytes).
- control: the records' fields as 20,000 scalar facts.

## 2026-11-23: does keys first shrink same-shape leaves?

**Question.** Does a table with the map keys first make neighbouring
same-shape records share more bytes in a leaf, and so shrink the tree?

**Method.** `composite_table_order size`: each corpus committed in one
transaction to a branch on the volatile provider, canonicalized, every
reachable node walked; a leaf is attributed to the ordering of its first
key. A, B, A, B, A, B in one sitting. The shared prefix is computed over
the composites in attribute order (attribute, then entity). The reachable
blocks are appended to one Chronicle file. Every B composite was checked to
read back canonically, to read the same entries and `get` results as A, and
to have A's merkle reference.

**Results.** Medians of three runs; spread of leaf bytes within a layout at
most 2,600 bytes.

| corpus | leaf A | leaf B | B / A | EAV B / A | AEV B / A | VAE B / A |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| records | 4,814,664 | 4,490,680 | 0.933 | 0.987 | 0.991 | 0.757 |
| records, 2 of 5 shared | 4,776,912 | 4,398,824 | 0.921 | 0.971 | 0.921 | 0.726 |
| syntax trees (stand-in) | 3,464,368 | 3,453,704 | 0.997 | 1.012 | 0.980 | 1.063 |
| mixed shapes and lists | 7,627,216 | 7,442,112 | 0.976 | 0.992 | 1.006 | 0.892 |
| control | 12,122,952 | 12,121,280 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.999 |

Per-ordering numbers are coarse: a leaf is about 48 KB and the one that
straddles the value and history orderings moves between them from run to
run (VAE and history each swing by up to 100 KB between runs of one
layout); the totals do not.

Shared prefix with the neighbour, attribute ordering:

| corpus | layout | mean bytes | median bytes | mean fraction | same header |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| records | A | 2.9 | 2 | 0.021 | 0.132 |
| records | B | 6.3 | 2 | 0.045 | 0.132 |
| records, 2 of 5 shared | A | 4.6 | 2 | 0.033 | 0.199 |
| records, 2 of 5 shared | B | 16.3 | 2 | 0.115 | 0.199 |
| syntax trees (stand-in) | A | 1.4 | 0 | 0.030 | 0.047 |
| syntax trees (stand-in) | B | 1.5 | 0 | 0.033 | 0.047 |
| mixed | A | 1.2 | 1 | 0.022 | 0.030 |
| mixed | B | 1.8 | 1 | 0.029 | 0.030 |

**Decision.** H1's mechanism does not hold in the attribute ordering: the
table header's `size` field differs for 87 percent of record neighbours, and
the shared prefix ends there under either layout. The gain is in the value
ordering, where byte order clusters equal headers. The size bar (10
percent) is not met.

## 2026-11-23: what keys first costs to write and read

**Question.** Is B within 5 percent of A to encode a record and to `get`
one field?

**Method.** `composite_table_order time`, 20,000 records of 2, 5 and 9
fields, A and B interleaved within each round; three sittings of 9, 15 and
15 rounds. A list of the same values' `item` is the control (no keys, so B
writes A's bytes).

**Results.** B / A of the medians, per sitting:

| measure | 2 fields | 5 fields | 9 fields |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| encode | 1.143, 1.111, 1.120 | 1.118, 1.126, 1.149 | 1.096, 1.151, 1.102 |
| `get` | 0.984, 0.988, 0.981 | 1.127, 1.115, 1.133 | 1.066, 1.072, 1.072 |
| list item (control) | 0.961, 0.998, 1.001 | 1.032, 1.117, 0.993 | 1.011, 1.012, 1.000 |

A key lookup is cheaper under B only on the 2-field map, whose 4-atom table
has no offsets: the scan stops after fewer atoms (about 2 percent). From 9
atoms the table has offsets and both layouts binary-search; B must first
find where its key run ends, the key of the map's last entry, and a body of
5 entries has no offsets, so it steps there.

**Decision.** Encoding misses the 5 percent bar in every sitting. B is
rejected.

Commits: see the branch `research/composite-table-order`.
